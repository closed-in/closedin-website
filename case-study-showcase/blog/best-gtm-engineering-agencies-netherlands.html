Best GTM engineering agencies in the Netherlands and Benelux (2026)

A GTM engineering agency builds the system that finds your buyers, enriches them, writes to them and feeds the replies into your CRM, instead of renting you a caller. In the Netherlands that market is small and young. This page profiles the ten firms we would put on a shortlist in November 2026, with the facts taken from their own websites and the Clay partner directory between 5 and 9 November, our own numbers included.

The short answer

If you want one firm to build and run outbound end to end (data, cold email, LinkedIn, CRM) for a B2B service or SaaS company in the Netherlands, the shortlist is closed:in (Amsterdam), Nebor (Amsterdam), LeadGem (Amsterdam), Saleslift Studio (Clay Studio tier) and Jetpacked (Dutch founder, works from Portugal). If you want a sales bureau with callers and a meeting guarantee, Match-day is the established name. If you want growth marketing and GTM engineering from one Amsterdam team, DGTL Motion. If your problem is demand rather than outbound, Dapper (Rotterdam) is the demand generation agency Dutch buyers compare against all of the above. For Belgium, Outbound Catalyst is the Clay partner based there. For a larger, UK-based outbound team that also runs European campaigns, SalesCaptain (London).

We are closed:in, so read our profile the way you would read any agency's self-listing: as a claim, with the case study links next to it so you can check. Every fact about another agency below comes from that agency's own site or from the Clay partner directory, checked between 5 and 9 November 2026. Where a firm does not publish something (most do not publish pricing), the text says so instead of guessing.

What a GTM engineering agency actually does

The term is two years old and already covers three different things. Before you compare prices, check which one a firm sells.

  1. A system they build and run for you. The agency owns the data work (Clay tables, waterfall enrichment, buying signals), the sending infrastructure (domains, inboxes, warmup, deliverability), the copy, the LinkedIn layer and the hand-off into your CRM. You get meetings and a weekly report. closed:in, SalesCaptain and Outbound Catalyst sit here; Nebor and Jetpacked offer it as one of two modes.
  2. A system they build that your team runs. The agency designs the Clay tables, signal tracking and CRM wiring, then your SDRs or founders send. Jetpacked's human-in-the-loop package, LeadGem's RevOps builds and DGTL Motion's GTM engineering are this shape.
  3. People, with tooling behind them. A sales bureau or outsourced SDR team that books meetings, often with a guarantee. Match-day sells this. It can be the right buy, but it is not engineering, and the price is driven by headcount rather than by complexity.

Dapper is a fourth thing: a demand generation agency (content, paid media, measurement) that creates inbound rather than outbound. It belongs on this list because Dutch buyers compare the two, and because the honest answer for some companies is demand, not cold email.

The difference matters when the engagement ends. In models one and two you should keep the Clay tables, domains and CRM automations. Ask every firm who owns the accounts. Nebor and Outbound Catalyst state on their sites that they build in your own accounts; we put the answer in writing in every proposal, and our pricing page lists what the retainer includes. If a firm cannot answer that question in one sentence, the system lives in their workspace and you are renting it.

How we chose and ranked

We did not rank by size or by who we like. The list is every firm we could verify as active, known in the Dutch market, Benelux-based or Benelux-serving, and either a Clay Solutions Partner or explicitly selling outbound or GTM systems to B2B companies, in the first week of November 2026. The order in the table follows four checks:

  • Verifiable base and team. A street address, named founders or a Clay directory entry. Two firms that appear on other "best agencies in the Netherlands" lists failed this test: WINGM's domain is for sale, and Utmost Agency has been renamed Nebor, so we list Nebor.
  • Stack transparency. Does the site name the tools? A firm that will not say whether it sends through Instantly, Smartlead or Lemlist usually has not decided, or does not want you to know what you are paying for.
  • Published pricing or published terms. Three firms on this list publish numbers: Jetpacked, Dapper and closed:in. Match-day publishes a meeting guarantee instead of a price; Nebor publishes the shape (setup fee plus monthly) without amounts. The rest ask you to book a call first, which is normal in this market and still worth noting.
  • Proof with numbers. Case studies with counts and dates, not logos. We link ours below; Nebor, Jetpacked, LeadGem, SalesCaptain, Match-day and Outbound Catalyst publish theirs on their own sites.

What we deliberately did not use: Clutch or Sortlist ratings (too few reviews in this niche to mean anything), LinkedIn follower counts, and the other agencies' own "best of" lists.

The ten agencies in detail

closed:in (Amsterdam)

How we work: two founders, Max on sales and strategy, Jurre on delivery. Nebor, formerly Utmost Agency (Amsterdam)

Nebor is run by two co-founders who describe themselves as neighbours in Amsterdam with eighteen years of sales between them: Andrew van Rossenberg, eight years in B2B business development including a stint at Gartner, who builds everything that brings customers in (outreach and ads), and Yannick Kok, ten years selling software to enterprise buyers, who took OneStream's EMEA business development team from last place to global number one in eighteen months and now builds the CRM side. The company rebranded from Utmost Agency to Nebor in 2026 and is listed in the Clay directory at Artisan tier.

The offer is wide: go-to-market strategy, outbound and sales automation, ABM and paid ads, and RevOps, built as one system in the client's own accounts. Clay scores accounts on signals (a funding round, a new hire, a tool change, a site visit), Claude or ChatGPT drafts the message, and every reply lands in HubSpot or Salesforce as one record per company. The site is explicit that the system stays with the client, and contrasts that with "a lead generation or Clay agency" where "you pay monthly and keep nothing", which tells you how they position against the rest of this list.

Pricing: implementation packages starting with a one-time setup fee and monthly monitoring; amounts not published. Nebor says its best clients compare it to hiring a sales rep, not to buying a tool.

Proof: the homepage cites a EUR 12M qualified pipeline from zero, 84 enterprise prospects in active conversation, 14 clients closed, and ten of ten major European trailer OEMs met; those figures are theirs, from their case study pages.

Best for: companies that want outbound, ABM and paid from the same team, in their own accounts, and have the internal capacity to run what gets built. Nebor also publishes its own "7 best GTM engineering agencies" list, so read that with the same caution you should read this one.

LeadGem (Amsterdam)

LeadGem is a boutique GTM team founded by Marijn, a senior growth hacker who previously worked at B2B marketing agencies including Endeavour and NoBears, and describes itself as one of the first Clay-certified agencies in the Netherlands (Artisan tier in the directory). The site, in English and Dutch, lists an address on Sumatrastraat in Amsterdam and leads with "RevOps and outbound automations": automating the lead-to-revenue process first, then multi-channel outbound on top, with industry pages for SaaS, manufacturing and recruitment.

The engineering is visible in the case studies. For Spacewell, a European smart-building software provider, LeadGem audited a 50,000-record HubSpot database, enriched and qualified 25,000 contacts, built lead scoring that runs on every new inbound lead, and surfaced more than 400 high-potential prospects, which the case frames as hundreds of hours of SDR research saved. The SaaS page describes the outbound side as multi-channel systems with web signals (tech stack, hiring, site visits) feeding personalised outreach at scale.

Pricing: not published. The entry point is a free GTM and revenue operations scan.

Best for: companies whose problem is as much the internal revenue process (CRM hygiene, routing, enrichment, attribution) as it is top of funnel. If your CRM is a mess, fix that first and LeadGem's RevOps angle makes sense. Less obvious if you want a fully managed sending operation.

Saleslift Studio (Netherlands)

Saleslift Studio is listed in the Clay directory at Studio tier, the highest tier among the Dutch firms we found, with a Netherlands location. The directory profile describes a go-to-market agency that helps B2B companies build revenue engines using data, AI, automation and outbound execution, and is candid that many companies implement Clay and still fail to generate pipeline because the strategy, targeting and messaging are missing; Saleslift positions itself as the bridge. One published case is a niche play: rather than targeting every sports club, the team used Clay to identify 267 hockey clubs in the Netherlands, scrape club details and logos, find decision-makers and structure the audience for outbound.

Their own website returned a 403 to our checks, so we could not verify services, team or pricing from the source.

Pricing: not published.

Best for: Clay-heavy data and strategy work where you want the highest-tier Clay partner in the country. Verify the service scope and who does the sending directly before shortlisting.

Jetpacked (Dutch-language, based in Portugal)

Jetpacked is Barrie Höster's GTM engineering practice. The site is in Dutch and English, the clients quoted are Dutch (a Shopify Plus agency, the SaaS scale-up Vormats, the engineering firm Devoteq), and the footer gives a Portuguese phone number and address, so expect remote delivery into the Netherlands. Jetpacked calls itself an official Clay Solutions Partner, one of the first 100, with more than 100 workflows shipped, and names the widest stack on this list: HubSpot, Clay, Apollo, n8n, Lemlist, Smartlead, Instantly, Leadinfo, OpenAI and Claude.

The product is a signal engine: a system that tracks what is happening at target accounts (a new leader with a fresh budget, a job post that mentions your problem, expansion abroad, a former customer's contact starting somewhere new, repeat visits to your site), combines the signals, and tells sales who to contact, when and with what opener. Three packages: signal-based outbound run for you, human in the loop where your reps own the conversation and the system does the research, and warm outbound that turns marketing signals into sales conversations.

Pricing: the only firm on this list besides closed:in and Dapper that publishes prices, with one caveat. The Dutch and English pages show different figures: signal-based outbound from EUR 3,000 per month on the Dutch page and EUR 5,000 per month on the English page; human in the loop at EUR 2,500 setup plus EUR 1,000 per month (Dutch) or EUR 3,000 setup plus EUR 1,000 per month (English); an ABM pilot of three months for EUR 3,000; and an hourly rate of EUR 85. Ask which applies. Note also that jetpacked.nl is a parked domain; the live site is jetpacked.io.

Proof: Insightology.ai from zero to 20+ demos a month; Vormats, a complete GTM engine on HubSpot and Clay with three times higher reply rates; Devoteq, a 30% reply rate and five warm leads a week on done-for-you outbound.

Best for: growth-stage B2B teams that have people to send and want the data and signal layer built properly. The EUR 1,000 per month run rate assumes your team does the sending.

Match-day is a B2B sales bureau rather than an engineering shop, and the largest Dutch firm on this list: its own site dates the start to 2015, puts the team at more than 70 specialists, and counts more than 100 organisations helped, with offices in Bunschoten-Spakenburg. Services are outbound sales, HubSpot implementation (added in 2023 alongside CRM and sales process work) and sales training, with industry pages for SaaS and software, IT services, manufacturing, finance and insurance, and trade associations. Of the 21 published client stories, eight are IT and SaaS, six business services, four manufacturing and three specialist.

The method is people first, tooling behind them: a sharp ideal customer profile, AI-built and enriched target lists from more than 50 data sources, then an outbound team that calls, emails and approaches via LinkedIn under one commercial storyline, with weekly reporting on who was approached, what came back and which conversations are booked. Match-day states that the fit is organisations with 25 or more staff and an average deal value of EUR 5,000 or more, that it guarantees a minimum of five qualified meetings a month and averages eight to twelve, and that first results come in four to six weeks.

The stack is broad and marketing-leaning rather than cold-email-leaning: HubSpot, Salesforce, AFAS and Pipedrive on the CRM side; n8n, Make and Zapier for automation; PhantomBuster and Customer.io for outreach and lifecycle; Mixpanel and Python for analytics and data engineering; plus paid social, SEA, SEO and CRO. Dapper (Rotterdam and Lisbon)

Dapper is a B2B demand generation agency, not an outbound agency, and the biggest company on this list by headcount: founded in 2019 in Rotterdam by Yvo Wander and Tycho Luijten as Dapper Rhinos, rebranded to Dapper in 2022 with a full commitment to B2B demand generation, now around 70 marketers across a Rotterdam headquarters on the Weena and a Lisbon office of about 20 people. Its site states it was named Europe's Best B2B Agency in 2025.

The services are content and creative, paid media and performance, data and measurement, and a "demand team" model where Dapper assembles a custom team of specialists (LinkedIn, Google, video, design, motion, email, AI) around the client's in-house marketing. Expertise pages cover B2B SaaS, B2B service and B2B hardware, and the case list includes Logitech, Ultimaker, Storyteq and Bluebird. One quoted client says that since working with Dapper prospects reach out to them "instead of relying on outbound", which is the honest framing: Dapper's job is to make outbound less necessary.

Pricing: published. A B2B marketing team from EUR 6,240 per month, with cost depending on the expertise mix and pace.

Best for: B2B companies with a marketing budget and a category to own, where inbound demand is the growth lever. If you are choosing between Dapper and an outbound agency, you are really choosing between demand and direct outreach; many companies above EUR 5M revenue end up needing both.

SalesCaptain (London)

SalesCaptain is a UK outbound and GTM agency at City Road in London that runs campaigns across the US, Canada, the UK, Europe and Asia, and says it has more than 80 demand generation team members and more than 60 client organisations. It calls itself an award-winning appointment setting agency and a Clay-certified agency ("#1 Enterprise Clay Partner" in its own words). Services are outbound sales, inbound-led outbound (qualifying MQLs by ICP and triggering outreach), CRM enrichment, LinkedIn social selling, and Clay and AI enablement for in-house teams.

The delivery model is a dedicated team per client: an account manager, two BDRs, two researchers and a copywriter, with bi-weekly syncs and Slack support, hand-vetting around 3,000 leads a month and scaling to 10,000 or more. The case studies are unusually specific for this market and give positive-reply counts with timeframes: 54 positive replies in two months for Sago Packaging's US engine, 127 positive replies and 20 meetings in one month for Humanbound's Europe-focused motion, 217 positive replies in six months from a small TAM for Tatton Investment Management, and USD 432K in new ARR for AdLib over 27 months.

Pricing: not published.

Best for: companies that want a larger, structured outbound team with researchers and BDRs, and are selling into the UK or US as well as the Benelux. Less local than the Dutch firms, and the sending volumes imply a wide TAM.

Outbound Catalyst (Belgium and Portugal)

The Clay directory lists Outbound Catalyst at Artisan tier with its base as "Europe (based in Belgium and Portugal)"; the legal entity on the website footer is a US LLC. Founder Olivier Tytgat started in SaaS sales in 2019, automated his own list building and sequences, joined several startups as the first salesperson and built the outbound process each time, then started Outbound Catalyst in 2023 after finding Clay. Today a team in Portugal maps markets across Europe while Olivier runs the company; the site counts more than 25 GTM projects delivered, more than 2,500 qualified meetings booked and 10,000 sales hours given back to reps.

The method is map, build, activate. Map: a market mapping engine that combines company registries, local sources and the usual databases into one picture of every account that fits, the buyers per account, tiers and buying signals, pushed into your CRM. Build: enrichment, scoring, signals, sequences copied from your best reps, and routing, wired into your own CRM so you own it. Activate: either Outbound Catalyst runs email and LinkedIn with a call on every positive reply, or your team runs it with the engine behind them. Testimonials on the directory page come from Belgian software companies, including one citing EUR 1.2M in pipeline across Belgium and the Netherlands in nine months.

Pricing: not published on the pages we checked (the site has a "how pricing works" page behind a call).

Best for: Belgian and Dutch B2B software companies that want the market mapped before any motion is picked, and the engine built in their own stack, with a founder who knows the Belgian market.

What these agencies cost, and how to read the numbers

Most firms on this list do not publish prices. From what is published and from what we quote ourselves, a done-for-you outbound system in the Netherlands runs between roughly EUR 3,000 and EUR 8,000 a month in 2026. Build-only packages where your team sends start lower: Jetpacked's EUR 1,000 per month run rate after setup is the lowest published number we found. A demand generation team at Dapper starts at EUR 6,240 per month. Hourly engineering, when offered, sits around EUR 85.

Three things move the price inside that range, in our experience of quoting it:

  • How hard your ICP is to find. Companies that sit cleanly in Apollo or Sales Navigator are cheap to list. Hospitality venues, boutique retailers, M&A advisors or niche industrials need custom Clay tables, Google Maps scraping and Claygent prompts. That is engineering time.
  • Volume and channels. A thousand emails a day needs more domains and inboxes than three hundred. Adding LinkedIn or signal-based plays adds work every week, not just at setup.
  • CRM depth. A Google Sheet of replies is cheap. Stage automation, lead scoring and reply routing into Close or HubSpot is not.

Compare that with a first SDR in the Netherlands: salary, tooling (Clay, a sender, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, data), ramp-up and management time. Our view: if you need the first meetings this quarter and nobody in-house has run cold email infrastructure before, an agency is cheaper. Once the system converts, hire and have the agency build what the hire runs.

Questions to ask on the first call

  1. Who owns the Clay tables, domains and inboxes when we stop?
  2. Which sending tool do you use, and how many inboxes per domain?
  3. Show me a weekly report from a current client, with names removed.
  4. How do you define a qualified meeting, and who decides?
  5. What happens in month one, week by week? (If the answer is "we start sending in week one", the domains are not warmed up.)
  6. Which of your cases is closest to my ICP, and can I speak to that client?

FAQ

What is the best GTM engineering agency in the Netherlands?

There is no neutral ranking in this niche; the lists that exist are written by agencies, including this one. For done-for-you outbound with published pricing, closed:in and Jetpacked are the two firms that tell you the price before the call. For RevOps-led builds, LeadGem. For outbound plus ABM in your own accounts, Nebor. For the highest Clay partner tier, Saleslift Studio. Pick by which model you want (run for you, built for you, or people), then by proof in your ICP.

Which agencies in the Netherlands are Clay Solutions Partners?

In the Clay partner directory in November 2026 we found closed:in (Amsterdam, Artisan), Nebor (Amsterdam, Artisan), LeadGem (Artisan) and Saleslift Studio (Studio). Jetpacked and SalesCaptain state partner status on their own sites. For Belgium, Outbound Catalyst (Artisan).

I am a B2B founder in Amsterdam. Who can build my outbound system end to end?

closed:in, Nebor and LeadGem are all Amsterdam-based and all build the full system (data, email, LinkedIn, CRM); DGTL Motion is also in Amsterdam with a marketing-first version of it. The practical difference: closed:in runs it for you on a monthly retainer with published pricing; Nebor builds it in your accounts and adds ABM and paid; LeadGem leads with RevOps. Book calls with two of them and ask the six questions above.

What does an outbound agency in the Netherlands cost per month?

Published numbers in November 2026: closed:in EUR 4,000 to 8,000 per month with no setup fee; Jetpacked EUR 3,000 to 5,000 per month, or EUR 2,500 to 3,000 setup plus EUR 1,000 per month when your team sends; Dapper from EUR 6,240 per month for a demand generation team. Everyone else quotes after a call. Expect EUR 3,000 to 8,000 for an outbound system that is run for you.

Should a 10-person B2B company hire an SDR or an outbound agency?

At ten people, usually the agency first. A Dutch SDR costs salary plus tooling and data, needs about three months to ramp, and needs someone to manage them. An agency gives you the system and the first meetings inside a month, and you can hire the SDR to run the system once you know it converts. The exception is a complex product that needs deep discovery on every call; then the SDR comes first and the agency builds the data layer.

Which agencies cover Belgium as well as the Netherlands?

Outbound Catalyst is the Clay partner based in Belgium. closed:in, Nebor and LeadGem run campaigns into Belgium from Amsterdam; Dutch and French copy is a question to ask each of them. SalesCaptain and Dapper cover Belgium from London and Rotterdam respectively.

Do these agencies work with Instantly and HeyReach?

closed:in runs Instantly for email and an Instantly Certified Expert is on the team; LinkedIn runs through a multi-account outreach tool alongside the sequence. Jetpacked names Instantly, Smartlead and Lemlist. Nebor, LeadGem and SalesCaptain name Clay and the CRM but not their sending tool on the pages we checked. Ask directly; the sending tool decides your deliverability setup.
